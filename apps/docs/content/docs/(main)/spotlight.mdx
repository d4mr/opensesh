Every working surface in opensesh — sessions, submissions review, speakers, resources, files, evaluation rounds — is built on one interaction pattern: the **spotlight**. Understand it once and every desk works the same way.

## How it works

A desk is a list. Selecting a row _spotlights_ it: the list compacts to make room, and a detail panel opens beside it with everything about that item — status, checklists, pending approvals, actions, history. The list never disappears and never loses your scroll position, so you stay oriented in the queue you were working through.

## Built for working through a queue

The pattern is tuned for the "forty things to get through" sessions that make up conference organizing:

- **`j` / `k` (or arrow keys)** move the spotlight down and up the list without touching the mouse — triage a whole queue from the keyboard.
- **`Escape` closes the panel**, scrolls the list back to the row you were on, and briefly highlights it — you never come back to a list that lost your place.
- **The URL carries the selection** as `?spotlight=<id>`. Copy the address bar and a teammate opens the exact same record in the exact same desk; browser back/forward walk your selection history.

<Callout title="Filters and the spotlight are independent">
  If the spotlighted item is filtered out of the current view, the panel says so ("Not in this
  view") and offers to clear the filters — it never silently switches your selection.
</Callout>

## The same panel does real work

Spotlight panels aren't read-only summaries. Depending on the desk, the panel is where you:

- Approve or reject a speaker's pending profile edit, shown as a side-by-side diff ([Speakers](/guides/speakers)).
- Track a session's readiness checklist and activity timeline, or cancel it ([Sessions](/guides/sessions)).
- Change workflow status, review deliverables, or jump into the content approval flow.
